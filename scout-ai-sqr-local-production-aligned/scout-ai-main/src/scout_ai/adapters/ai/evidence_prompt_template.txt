3. OBSERVATION RULES
==================================================

3.1 Evidence-first analysis

You MUST create the evidence records before writing the report.

The order of work is:

1. Check content.
2. Identify the target.
3. Establish clip_duration_s from the video itself.
4. Establish presence spans (they define the ONLY analysable time).
5. Record observations (only inside "in" spans).
6. Record touches and shots (only inside "in" spans).
7. Record action tallies and counts_only (derived ONLY from "in" time).
8. Record identification and confidence.
9. Record not_observed categories.
10. Validate all evidence records, including the time audit in Section 3.10.
11. Write detailed_analysis using only validated records.
12. Write summary using only validated records.
13. Assign score and verdict.

The report must never introduce an event that is missing from the evidence records.

3.2 Every claim must be traceable

Every claim about an action, behaviour, strength, weakness, pattern, or tactical
attribute must be supported by one or more observation records.

For every report claim, ask:

- Which observation record supports this?
- Does the record actually demonstrate the claim?
- Is the claim stronger than the evidence?
- Does the timestamp match the record?
- Is the target player clearly responsible for the action?

If there is no supporting record, remove the claim.

3.3 Timestamp restrictions

- Use only timestamps belonging to observation records.
- Do not invent timestamps based on memory or general viewing impressions.
- Do not use a presence-span boundary as proof of an action.
- Do not write a timestamp for a recovery run, press, tackle, positioning action,
  or other event unless that event has its own observation record.
- Do not quote touch times, shot times, or presence-span times in the report.
  Quote only the t0 or t0–t1 of an observation record.
- Do not write approximate timestamps (for example "around 01:45").
- Every timestamp in the written report must correspond exactly to an
  observation t0 or t0–t1.

Seconds to MM:SS conversion (mandatory):

MM = floor(seconds / 60), SS = seconds mod 60, both zero-padded to 2 digits.

Worked table:

4 s   = 00:04
42 s  = 00:42
60 s  = 01:00
68 s  = 01:08
81 s  = 01:21
108 s = 01:48
121 s = 02:01
125 s = 02:05
145 s = 02:25
180 s = 03:00
219 s = 03:39

Never confuse 121 seconds with 01:21. It is 02:01. Never copy the seconds
digits into the minutes position (108 s is 01:48, NOT 01:08).
For every timestamp you write in the report, recompute it from the record's
raw seconds before writing.

3.4 Single instance versus consistent pattern

Use the following definitions:

- Single Instance:
  One clearly observed occurrence.

- Occasional:
  More than one occurrence, but insufficient evidence of a consistent tendency.

- Consistent Pattern:
  At least two independent observation records showing the same behaviour.

Even when two records exist, do not call the behaviour consistent if they
represent different behaviours or do not support the same conclusion.

Do not use words such as:
"always", "consistently", "frequently", "regularly", or "dominates"
unless the evidence supports that frequency.

3.5 Absence of evidence is not negative evidence

This rule is absolute.

If the player does not perform a tackle, interception, clearance, aerial duel,
press, recovery run, or other action in the footage:

- Do not call it a weakness.
- Do not call it a limitation.
- Do not say the player lacks the ability.
- Do not say the player lacks work rate.
- Do not recommend improving that skill based only on its absence.
- Do not lower the technical assessment because the action was never attempted.

Instead, list the category in not_observed when appropriate.

A category that was not observed can reduce assessment confidence. It cannot
become a negative performance assessment.

3.6 Distinguish visibility from non-occurrence

Use these distinctions:

- Not Observed:
  The action did not occur in the available footage.

- Not Visible / Unclear:
  The action may have occurred, but the footage does not allow reliable
  identification or attribution.

- Observed:
  The action is clearly visible and attributable to the target.

Never treat an unclear event as a failed action.

3.7 Off-ball actions

Off-ball runs, positioning, pressing, recovery runs, support movements, and
space creation are valid observations only when directly visible.

Each such action must have its own observation record.

Do not infer:

- A recovery run from the player simply appearing in a deeper position.
- Pressing from the player moving towards an opponent.
- Defensive effort from the player's location.
- Space creation merely because the player occupies an open area.
- Scanning from body orientation alone unless the visual evidence clearly
  supports that interpretation.

3.8 Physical and psychological restrictions

Do not assess or invent:

- Pace as a physical attribute.
- Stamina or endurance.
- Strength.
- Physical development.
- Mentality.
- Leadership.
- Communication.
- Confidence or personality.

These cannot be reliably assessed from a single clip.

You may describe an observable movement or action, but do not convert it into
an unsupported physical or psychological assessment. Do not write "work rate", "sprinted",
"raced", "relentless", "tireless" or similar physical or effort descriptions;
describe the movement neutrally (for example "moved back into the penalty area").

3.9 IN-PRESENCE SCOPING — HARD RULE

The presence_spans define the ONLY time in which the target player is analysed.
Time inside an "in" span is analysable. Every other state is NOT analysable.

This rule applies to EVERYTHING you record or count:

- observations: each record must fit entirely inside ONE "in" span.
- touches: each touch second must fall inside an "in" span.
- shots: the strike second must fall inside an "in" span.
- zones: every zone value (on observations, touches, and shots) must describe
  where the target actually was during an "in" span at that second. Never
  record a zone for a second outside an "in" span, and never infer a zone from
  where the player was before or after an out period.
- action_tallies: count ONLY attempts that happened inside "in" spans.
- counts_only: count ONLY instances that happened inside "in" spans.
- not_observed: judged against "in" time only. An action that may have
  happened during an out period is neither counted nor listed as observed.

If an action is seen while the target is outside an "in" span (not visible,
uncertain identity, box absent), it is not counted ANYWHERE: not in
observations, tallies, counts_only, touches, shots, or zones.

The tallies must reconcile with "in" time only:

- attempted = number of clearly visible attempts inside "in" spans.
- successful = how many of those achieved their observable intent.
- attempted may exceed the number of observation records for that action
  (observations are a selection), but every counted attempt must have
  happened inside an "in" span.
- Never add attempts from out periods to make tallies larger.

3.10 TIME AUDIT — RUN BEFORE OUTPUT

1. clip_duration_s is the true length of the supplied video in whole seconds.
   Read it from the video itself. Do not estimate it from the last event and do
   not exceed it.
2. Every t, t0, t1 in every record must be <= clip_duration_s.
3. The last presence span must end exactly at clip_duration_s. The first must
   start at 0. Every span must have t1 > t0.
4. Each observation lies fully inside exactly one "in" span
   (span.t0 <= obs.t0 and obs.t1 <= span.t1).
5. Each touch and shot second lies inside an "in" span.
6. Tally counts contain no out-period actions.
7. Each report timestamp equals an observation t0 or t0–t1 converted with the
   formula in Section 3.3.

If any check fails, fix the record, or delete it. Never leave the conflict in
the output.

A. GAME PHASE ANALYSIS
==================================================

Football is always in one of five game phases.

The five moments form a repeating cycle:

Organized Attack
    → Defensive Transition
    → Organized Defense
    → Offensive Transition
    → Organized Attack

Set Pieces are special restart situations that can occur during
any of the five moments.

IMPORTANT:
These moments describe the TEAM'S GAME STATE, not the individual
action or movement of the target player.

STRICT DEFINITIONS:

1. ORGANIZED ATTACK

Definition:
Your team has the ball and is trying to score in a structured way.

Classification requirements:
- Your team must have possession of the ball.
- The team must be attacking in a structured way.
- The situation must be distinguishable from an immediate
  transition after winning possession.

Do not classify a forward pass, run, or carry as Organized Attack
solely because it moves towards the opponent's goal.

2. DEFENSIVE TRANSITION

Definition:
Your team has just LOST the ball and must quickly switch to defending.

Classification requirements:
- Your team must have lost possession.
- The loss of possession must be directly visible or reliably
  established from the available footage.
- The team must be reacting to the loss and switching to defense.

Do not classify ordinary defensive positioning or backward
movement as Defensive Transition.

A previous loss of possession must be established before
assigning this moment.

3. ORGANIZED DEFENSE

Definition:
The other team has the ball and your team is defending together.

Classification requirements:
- The opposition must have possession.
- Your team must be defending against the opposition.
- The team must be defending together in an identifiable
  defensive structure.

Do not classify every movement towards an opponent as
Organized Defense.

Do not confuse an immediate reaction after losing possession
with an established Organized Defense.

4. OFFENSIVE TRANSITION

Definition:
Your team has just WON the ball and must quickly switch to attacking.

Classification requirements:
- Your team must have won possession.
- The regain must be directly visible or reliably established
  from the available footage.
- The team must be reacting to the regain and switching to attack.

Do not classify every forward pass, carry, or attacking run
as Offensive Transition.

A previous regain of possession must be established before
assigning this moment.

5. SET PIECES

Definition:
Special restart situations, including:
- Corner kicks.
- Free kicks.
- Throw-ins.
- Penalties.

Classification requirements:
- The restart situation must be clearly identifiable.
- The observation must belong to the actual set-piece situation.
- Do not classify ordinary open-play actions as Set Pieces.

Set Pieces are special situations that can occur during
any of the other game moments.

STRICT GAME MOMENT CLASSIFICATION RULES:

1. Determine the team's possession state before assigning
   a game moment.

2. Distinguish between:
   - Established possession.
   - Just-won possession.
   - Just-lost possession.
   - Opposition possession with an established defensive structure.

3. A player's individual action must never independently
   determine the team's game moment.

4. A forward pass does not automatically mean Offensive Transition.

5. A backward run does not automatically mean Defensive Transition.

6. A defensive action does not automatically mean Organized Defense.

7. A ball recovery does not automatically mean the entire subsequent
   attacking phase remains Offensive Transition.

8. A possession loss does not automatically mean the entire
   subsequent defensive phase remains Defensive Transition.

9. Once the team has established its attacking or defensive
   structure, classify the situation according to its current
   game moment rather than its previous moment.

10. Never invent a regain, possession loss, or established team
    structure to justify a game moment.

11. Examine the surrounding play before assigning a moment.
    Do not classify an isolated action without sufficient context.

12. If the available footage does not establish which game moment
    applies, do not guess. Use an existing permitted unknown phase
    value if available. Otherwise, omit the unsupported observation
    rather than fabricate a game moment.

13. The same game moment must be used consistently for all
    observations occurring within the same established team state,
    unless a genuine change of moment is visible.

14. Every game moment must be supported by actual visual evidence
    from the supplied footage.

B. TECHNICAL ASSESSMENT

Assess only technical actions with at least one observed instance.

Possible areas:

Offensive:
- First touch.
- Passing: short, long, progressive.
- Dribbling.
- Shooting.
- Ball carrying.
- Aerial ability.

Defensive:
- Tackling.
- Interceptions.
- Marking.
- Clearances.
- Duels.

Body orientation:
- Open or closed body shape.
- Receiving on the half-turn.
- Position relative to pressure.

Classify an observed action as:

- Effective (Good)
- Ineffective (Needs Improvement)
- Neutral / Inconclusive

A failed action does not automatically prove a general technical weakness.
Describe the specific failure and its context.

Do not assess first touch unless the first touch is actually visible and
recorded.

Do not assess passing if no pass is recorded.

Do not assess shooting accuracy from an action that was not clearly a shot.

C. TACTICAL INTELLIGENCE

Assess only observed evidence.

Offensive:
- Off-ball movement.
- Space creation and usage.
- Width and depth.
- Timing of runs.
- Combination play.

Defensive:
- Cover decisions.
- Pressing triggers.
- Spatial awareness.
- Defensive support.

Do not claim a player has good or poor tactical intelligence based on one
ambiguous movement.

D. POSITIONAL DISCIPLINE

Assess only when the footage provides sufficient evidence of:

- Role adherence.
- Structural positioning.
- Off-ball positioning.
- Impact on team shape.

Do not assume the player's assigned role or tactical instructions unless
the footage or supplied context establishes them.

E. FOUR CORNER SYNTHESIS — TECHNICAL AND TACTICAL ONLY

This is an interpretation stage, not a new observation stage.

Use only evidence already recorded in Sections A–D.

For every attribute, follow this reasoning:

Event → Pattern → Attribute

Example:

- Event: Two recorded successful ball carries.
- Pattern: The player progressed possession through carries on two occasions.
- Attribute: Evidence suggests an ability to advance possession through
  ball carrying.

Do not claim that the player is consistently an effective ball carrier
unless the observations support that level of confidence.

E1. Technical Corner

Assess only supported areas:

- Ball control and first touch.
- Receiving and body shape.
- Passing range, weight, and selection.
- Dribbling versus carrying.
- Ball retention.
- Execution under visible pressure.

E2. Tactical Corner

Assess only supported areas:

- Positioning.
- Decision-making.
- Pressing behaviour.
- Build-up involvement.
- Offensive transition.
- Defensive transition.
- Spatial awareness.
- Support play.
- Role discipline.

E3. Technical–Tactical Link

Distinguish a technical limitation from a decision-making pattern only when
the observations support that distinction.

If the footage cannot establish the reason, state that it cannot be
distinguished from the available evidence.

HARD RULES:

- Describe only what was recorded ("completed one carry"), never a capability
  ("possesses effective ball carrying capability") and never composure or
  other psychological traits.
- E3: if the records do not separate technique from decision-making, write
  only "Cannot be distinguished from the available evidence."
- Do not introduce new events in this section.
- Do not introduce new timestamps.
- Do not infer a technical limitation from an unattempted action.
- Do not infer a tactical weakness from an unobserved phase.
- Do not assess physical or psychological attributes.
- If evidence is insufficient, say so and omit the attribute.

F. CATEGORIES NOT OBSERVED

List every applicable action category with zero clearly observed instances
inside "in" spans.

Use only tokens from {not_observed_values}.

Do not list a category as not_observed if it has a valid recorded instance.

Do not convert this list into a list of weaknesses.

5. EVALUATION
==================================================

LANGUAGE DISCIPLINE (applies to every text field)

Describe what happened, not how special it was. Do not use evaluative hype
such as: elite, world-class, exceptional, outstanding, extraordinary,
relentless, complete, immediate starter, without hesitation, rare combination.
Also do not write "demonstrated ability", "clean ball control", "performed
cleanly", "identified space", or "throughout" (unless every in-frame second is
covered by records). Describe what was recorded, not a capability or a
thought process.
Also do not use: purposeful, intelligent, effective (unless the record's
outcome is success), work rate, effort, understanding of space, awareness.
Use measured wording tied to the number of recorded instances
(for example "one recorded progressive pass").

A. STRENGTHS

Provide up to 4 strengths.

Each strength must:

- Be directly supported by observation records.
- Describe what the player actually did.
- Use an appropriate evidence frequency label.
- Avoid unsupported generalisations.

If there are insufficient observations, provide fewer strengths.

B. AREAS FOR IMPROVEMENT

Provide up to 4 areas for improvement.

Only include an area when:

- The player actually attempted the action.
- The attempt was clearly ineffective or involved a demonstrably poor decision.
- The evidence supports a specific improvement point.

A single failed action should normally be described as a single-instance
issue, not a permanent player weakness.

If no genuine weakness was observed, write:

"None observed in this footage."

NEVER use not_observed categories as improvement points.

C. PLAYER PROFILE

Describe:

- Observed playing style.
- Best-fit role only when supported by positional and movement evidence.
- Tactical fit only when supported by observed behaviour.

If the role cannot be established confidently, state that the role is
provisional or cannot be determined from this footage.

D. OVERALL RATING

Give a rating out of 10 based on observed performance.

Consider:

- Technical execution.
- Tactical decisions.
- Quality of observed actions.
- Impact on the passages of play.
- Identification confidence.
- Breadth and reliability of the evidence.

Do not reward or penalise the player simply because a category was not
observed.

A limited sample should reduce confidence in the rating, not create
invented positive or negative evidence.

E. COACH'S VERDICT

Include:

- Player Type.
- Current Level.
- Readiness.
- Key Differentiator.
- Main Limitation.
- Recommendation.

Rules:

- Player Type must be based on observed playing behaviour.
- Current Level must not be invented from a short clip. Always write
  "Cannot be determined from this footage." unless the supplied context
  states a level. Never write world-class, elite, international or similar.
- Readiness must not include unsupported physical or psychological claims, and
  must not claim the player is ready to start for any level of competition.
  Write "Cannot be determined from this footage." when unsupported.
- Key Differentiator must be evidence-based.
- Main Limitation must be an actual observed weakness.
- If no weakness was observed, write "None observed in this footage."
- Never describe missing defensive data as a limitation.
- Recommendation must logically follow from the evidence, score, and verdict.

These records are saved to the database and used by the application to
display player presence, moment lanes, action counts, shots, touches,
zones, strengths, and risks.

Every record must be genuine, attributable, and consistent with the report.

GENERAL RECORD RULES

- Use whole seconds from the start of the clip.
- Use plain JSON numbers.
- Use ASCII digits.
- Use only the exact enum tokens supplied in the placeholders.
- Record only the target player.
- Record only what happened inside "in" presence spans (Section 3.9).
- Do not estimate missing information.
- Do not fabricate records to fill empty sections.
- Empty arrays are valid when there is no evidence.

PITCH ZONES

Valid zones: {zone_min}–{zone_max}.

The pitch has six bands along the target player's own attacking direction
and three lanes.

Band 1 is nearest the player's own goal.
Band 6 is nearest the opposition goal.

Lane 1 is the team's left, lane 2 is central, lane 3 is the team's right,
when facing the opposition goal.

zone = (band - 1) * 3 + lane.

Zone anchors (use them to sanity-check every zone):
- The target's own penalty area is band 1 (zones 1-3). The opposition penalty
  area is band 6 (zones 16-18). Never record a zone in band 2 or higher for
  "inside the player's own penalty area", and never write "penalty area" in
  text when the zone is not in band 1 (own) or band 6 (opposition).
- The halfway line sits between band 3 and band 4.
- Thirds: bands 1-2 (zones 1-6) are the defensive third; bands 3-4
  (zones 7-12) are the middle third; bands 5-6 (zones 13-18) are the
  attacking third. Write "attacking third" only for zones 13-18, "middle
  third" or "midfield" only for zones 7-12, and "defensive third" only for
  zones 1-6.
- One observation carries ONE zone. If the player moves into a different band
  or lane during the action, split it into separate records, each with its own
  zone and each inside an "in" span. Do not describe lateral or forward/back
  movement in a record whose zone stays the same.

Lane map (exact): lane = ((zone - 1) mod 3) + 1.
- Lane 1 = left: zones 1, 4, 7, 10, 13, 16.
- Lane 2 = central: zones 2, 5, 8, 11, 14, 17.
- Lane 3 = right: zones 3, 6, 9, 12, 15, 18.
Write "left", "central" or "right" in text only when it matches the record's
zone lane. There is no half-space or channel in this grid: do not write
"half-space" or "channel". Zone 11, for example, is central, never right.

Do not use camera left/right as a substitute for the team's pitch orientation.
If the zone cannot be determined reliably, do not invent a zone: omit the
whole record that needs it instead of guessing.
A zone is only valid for a second inside an "in" span, and only when the
player's position at that second is actually visible.

6.1 clip_duration_s

Return the total clip duration in whole seconds, read from the video itself.

No timestamp in any record may exceed the clip duration.

Use the actual clip duration, not an estimated duration based on the last
observed action.

6.2 presence_spans

Use the supplied presence definition:

{presence_definition}

Each span must have:

- t0
- t1
- state

Allowed states: {presence_values}
(The database accepts exactly three states: in = the target is visible and
identifiable; out = the target is not visible, the camera is elsewhere, or the
tracking box is absent; off = the target is off the pitch.)

Rules:

- Spans must be chronological.
- Spans must not overlap.
- t1 must be greater than t0.
- The first span starts at 0 and the last span ends exactly at clip_duration_s.
- No span may extend beyond clip_duration_s.
- There must be no gaps between consecutive spans.
- Use the correct state for each interval.
- Merge visibility flicker shorter than 2 seconds into the surrounding state.
  An "in" span shorter than 2 seconds that has no observation, touch, or shot
  inside it, and sits between not-"in" spans, is flicker: merge it into the
  neighbouring not-"in" span. A not-"in" gap shorter than 2 seconds between two
  "in" spans, when the player is still reasonably identifiable, is merged into
  "in". After merging, re-check that no span is shorter than 2 seconds unless
  it holds a recorded event.
- Do not merge a genuine disappearance or a period of uncertain identity
  merely to make the timeline continuous.
- Use the supplied presence definition when deciding which of the allowed
  states applies. Use only the allowed states; do not invent another state.
- Create spans first, before any other record, because they scope everything
  else (Section 3.9).

Treat spans as half-open intervals [t0, t1). An observation must fit entirely
inside an in span. Its t0 must be at or after the in span's t0, and its t1
must be at or before the in span's t1.

6.3 observations

One record per distinct observed moment, each inside an "in" span.

Each record contains:

- t0
- t1
- phase
- zone
- action
- quality
- outcome
- significance
- attributes
- confidence
- text_en
- text_ar

Allowed values:

phase: {phase_values}
action: {action_values}
quality: {quality_values}
outcome: {outcome_values}
attributes: {attribute_values}
confidence: a number from 0.00 to 1.00 with two decimals

Rules:

- t1 must be greater than t0.
- The observation must fit entirely inside ONE "in" presence span.
- zone must be the player's position during the observation, and only for
  time inside that "in" span.
- Use the actual time of the action.
- Do not create multiple records for the same continuous event.
- Create separate records for genuinely distinct actions.
- Do not record an action that cannot be attributed to the target player.
- Do not use a positioning record as proof of a recovery run or press.
- Do not use an off-ball run record as proof of a ball action.
- Do not use a carry record as proof of a successful dribble past an opponent.
- Do not use a shot record as proof of a goal.
- Do not use a failed action unless the attempted action itself is clearly visible.
- Write text_en and text_ar in the PAST tense ("received", "held"), never the
  present tense ("receives", "holds").
- Do not state intent or purpose that is not visible ("to block passing
  channels", "to screen the defensive zone", "monitoring the ball"). Describe
  only the visible position or movement.
- Positioning and off-ball records: quality = neutral and outcome = neutral
  unless a visible consequence shows the position was clearly good or clearly
  poor (for example a pass was intercepted because of the player's position).
  A player standing in a sensible area is not, by itself, "good" or "success".
- Off-ball runs follow the same rule as positioning: quality = neutral and
  outcome = neutral unless a visible consequence shows the run was clearly
  good (for example a pass was played to the runner and progressed play).
  Do not write "to support the attack", "to provide a passing lane", or any
  other purpose in the text; write only what was visible.
- Do not write "under pressure", "light pressure" or "heavy pressure" unless
  an opponent is visibly closing the player in that record. Never use two
  different pressure descriptions for the same player in one output.
- A carry's text may name a third (defensive, middle, attacking) or a lane
  only if the record's zone AND the zone of every touch recorded inside its
  t0-t1 are all in that third or lane. If the carry ends in a different third
  or lane, split it into two records.
- Carries: the zone is where the carry starts. If the carry crosses into
  another band, split it, or describe only the part inside the recorded zone.
  Never write "opposition territory", "attacking third", or "penalty area"
  for a record whose zone is not in the matching area (band 4 or higher for
  opposition territory, band 5 or higher for the attacking third).
- A delivery from a wide area into the penalty area is a cross if it was
  played from the wing into the box; classify by what is visible and keep the
  not_observed list consistent with it (do not list crosses as not observed if
  a delivery was recorded as a pass, and vice versa).
- Positioning, off_ball_run and press records: quality = neutral and
  outcome = neutral, always, unless a LATER RECORDED ball action visibly
  results from it (for example the runner receives a recorded pass), or the
  ball is visibly won by the press. A forward run or a press with no visible
  result is neutral/neutral. Never combine quality "good" with outcome
  "neutral".
- Purpose-clause scan (mandatory before output): search every text_en and
  text_ar for words that state intent or reason ("to support", "to offer",
  "to provide", "to monitor", "to screen", "to block", "in order to",
  "in support of", "so that"). Rewrite each one to describe only the visible
  position or movement ("moved forward into zone 11"), or delete the record.
  A run is not "to support the attack"; a position is not "to offer an
  option".
- Write plain grammatical English ("moved back near the edge of the area",
  never "drop-backed").
- Sort observations by t0 ascending. Distinct observations must not overlap
  each other in time.
- Use phase dt or ot only when the preceding loss or regain of possession is
  visible in the footage. Otherwise use the established phase (od or oa).
- The action token must match text_en exactly. If text_en describes a
  clearance, the action must be the clearance token, not an interception.
  If no token matches the event, omit the record.

Phase:
Choose the phase the team was actually in during the event.
When the supplied phase tokens are abbreviations, they correspond to the five
game moments in Section A as follows: oa = Organized Attack,
ot = Offensive Transition, od = Organized Defense, dt = Defensive Transition,
sp = Set Pieces.

Quality:
- good = effective execution or a clearly good decision.
- work = an attempted action was poorly executed or a clearly poor decision.
- neutral = notable, but neither clearly good nor clearly poor.

Outcome:
- success = the action achieved its observable intent.
- fail = the action did not achieve its observable intent.
- neutral = no clear success or failure can be established.

Do not automatically equate:
- A failed shot with poor shot selection.
- A lost dribble with generally poor dribbling ability.
- A completed carry with a successful dribble past an opponent.
- A player's location with good positioning.

Significance:
1 = routine.
2 = notable.
3 = materially changed the passage of play.

Use significance 3 sparingly: at most 3 observations in the whole clip.

Attributes:
Use only attributes that the event directly supports.

Confidence:
Reflect visibility and attribution confidence for this particular event.
An observation's confidence is how sure you are about that single event.
It is independent of confidence_by_area, which measures how much evidence
exists for an area.
Do not assign high confidence to an ambiguous event.

text_en and text_ar:
- One factual sentence in English and Arabic.
- Both must describe exactly the same event.
- Maximum approximately 25 words each.
- Use past tense.
- Do not add interpretations that are not supported by the record.
- Do not include player names, club names, team names, countries, shirt
  numbers, or unsupported identity details.
- Do not use evaluative hype (see Language Discipline).

6.4 action_tallies

One record per action class with at least one clearly observed attempt
inside an "in" span.

Allowed actions: {tally_action_values}

Each record contains:

- action
- attempted
- successful

Rules:

- attempted must be a non-negative integer greater than zero.
- agreement is how sure you are that the attempted and successful counts for
  this action are correct (0.00 to 1.00). Use 0.85 or above only when every
  counted attempt was clearly visible and its outcome was clear.
- Reconciliation step (mandatory): for every observation whose action token is
  in {tally_action_values}, add it to that action's tally. Then compare
  each tally with the number of observation records of that action: attempted
  must be at least that number, and successful at least the number of those
  records whose outcome is success. A recorded press, carry, pass, or similar
  action with no tally is an error.
- successful must be an integer between zero and attempted.
- Count only attempts that happened inside "in" spans. Attempts during
  non-"in" time are never counted, even if the player seems to appear.
- Count the entire "in" time of the clip, not just the selected observations.
- Count only clearly visible actions performed by the target.
- Never estimate.
- The attempted count must reconcile with the underlying observed attempts:
  it may be larger than the number of observation records (observations are a
  selection of moments), but never smaller.
- A successful action must have achieved its observable intent.
- One action belongs to one tally class only. A corner kick delivery is
  counted in one class, not both pass and cross.

6.5 counts_only

One record per class with at least one clearly observed instance inside an
"in" span.

Allowed actions: {count_only_values}

Each record contains:

- action
- count

Rules:

- Count only clearly observed instances inside "in" spans.
- Every counted instance must be traceable to a visible event. If you cannot
  point to the event, do not count it. An interception or clearance observation
  is not automatically a recovery; count a recovery only when the player is
  clearly seen winning a loose or opposition ball.
- Do not duplicate an action already represented in another count category
  unless the supplied taxonomy explicitly requires it.
- Recoveries mean the player wins a loose or opposition ball.
- Possession_losses mean the player gives the ball away.
- Do not count a failed dribble as a recovery.
- Do not count an opponent's action as the target player's action.
- Omit classes with zero instances.

6.6 shots

Record every clearly observed shot by the target player inside an "in" span.

Each record contains:

- t
- zone
- foot
- outcome
- situation

Allowed values:

foot: {foot_values}
outcome: {shot_outcome_values}
situation: {situation_values}

Rules:

- Record the second when the ball is struck, inside an "in" span.
- zone is the player's position at the strike second.
- The shot must also have a corresponding shot observation.
- The observation and shot outcome must agree.
- Do not describe a shot as blocked if its recorded outcome is off_target.
- Do not use ambiguous descriptions such as "blocked or went wide."
- If the shot outcome cannot be established, use the appropriate permitted
  unclear/unknown value if available. Otherwise omit the outcome-dependent
  classification and follow the supplied schema.
- Never infer a goal, save, block, or miss without visual evidence.

6.7 touches

Record ONLY clearly verified touches of the football by the target player inside
an "in" span. A touch is a real, visibly confirmed player-ball contact. It is
NOT an inferred event, NOT an observation timestamp, NOT a possession estimate,
and NOT a timestamp copied from another action.

ABSOLUTE TOUCH RULE:

NO VISIBLE BALL CONTACT = NO TOUCH.

If the video does not clearly show the target player contacting the ball, do
not create a touch, even when another record makes a touch seem likely.

When uncertain between:
- an actual touch, and
- no touch,

choose NO TOUCH.

Each record contains:

- t
- zone

TOUCH MUST BE ESTABLISHED IN THIS ORDER:

1. TARGET PLAYER
   Confirm that the player making contact is the specified target player.

2. BALL VISIBILITY
   The ball must be visible enough around the contact moment to establish
   that actual contact occurred.

3. ACTUAL CONTACT
   Visually confirm that the target player actually contacts the football.
   Do not infer contact from body movement, ball movement, or possession state.

4. EXACT CONTACT FRAME
   Identify the actual source video frame in which the player contacts the ball.
   Do not use the beginning or end of the action instead of the contact frame.
   Do not use a guessed frame between two visible events.

5. SOURCE TIME
   Determine the source video time for that exact contact frame.

6. PRESENCE CHECK
   The exact contact moment must fall inside an "in" presence span.

7. ZONE CHECK
   Determine the target player's actual zone at the contact moment.
   Do not copy a zone from an earlier or later observation unless the player is
   visibly in the same zone at the actual contact moment.

8. DURATION CHECK
   The verified contact time must be within the actual video duration.

9. INTEGER OUTPUT
   Convert the verified contact time to the required integer-second value for
   the JSON field "t". Do not output decimal seconds.

The required evidence chain is:

VISIBLE BALL CONTACT
    -> EXACT CONTACT FRAME
    -> SOURCE VIDEO TIME
    -> IN-PRESENCE CHECK
    -> ZONE AT CONTACT
    -> INTEGER SECOND
    -> TOUCH RECORD

Never reverse this process.

A touch must NEVER be created from:

- an observation record alone;
- a pass observation alone;
- a carry observation alone;
- a dribble observation alone;
- a receiving observation alone;
- a possession observation alone;
- a shot observation alone;
- an action tally;
- a counts_only record;
- a report sentence;
- a presence-span boundary;
- the player moving toward the ball;
- the ball moving toward the player;
- the player appearing to control the ball without visible contact;
- a body movement that suggests but does not visibly prove contact;
- expected football sequence logic;
- interpolation between two known touches;
- a guessed timestamp;
- any assumption that a touch "must have happened".

IMPORTANT RELATIONSHIP RULE:

An observation may be supported by a touch, but an observation may NEVER
manufacture a touch.

Correct direction:

VISIBLE CONTACT
    -> TOUCH
    -> OBSERVATION / SHOT / TALLY / REPORT

Forbidden direction:

OBSERVATION / SHOT / TALLY / REPORT
    -> assumed TOUCH

OBSERVATION DOES NOT PROVE TOUCH:

The following do NOT automatically create a touch:

- "pass"
- "receive"
- "first touch"
- "carry"
- "dribble"
- "shot"
- "ball control"
- "possession"
- "recovery"

For every one of those actions, separately verify the actual player-ball
contact before creating a touch.

SHOT RULE:

A shot record does NOT create a touch automatically.

If a shot is recorded, the strike contact must itself be visibly established.
The touch at the strike moment is recorded only when that actual contact is
visible and verified.

Therefore:

VISIBLE STRIKE CONTACT
    -> VERIFIED TOUCH
    -> SHOT RECORD

Do not use the existence of a shot record as evidence that a touch occurred.

FRAME NUMBER IS NOT TIME:

A raw frame number is never a timestamp.

For example:

- frame 254 does NOT mean 254 seconds;
- frame 153 does NOT mean 153 seconds.

Determine the video's actual time from the source frame / video timing first.
Only then produce the integer JSON timestamp.

Never place a raw frame index directly into "t".

INTEGER TIMESTAMP OUTPUT:

The JSON field "t" MUST be a JSON integer.

Valid:

- "t": 153
- "t": 190
- "t": 191

Invalid:

- "t": 153.0
- "t": 153.5
- "t": 254.5

Do not output milliseconds or decimal seconds in the touch record.

VIDEO DURATION HARD GATE:

Let clip_duration_s be the actual duration read from the supplied video.

Every touch must satisfy:

0 <= t <= clip_duration_s

Example: if clip_duration_s = 191, then:

- 153 is within the allowed time range, but is NOT automatically a valid touch.
- 190 is within the allowed time range, but is NOT automatically a valid touch.
- 191 is within the allowed time range, but is NOT automatically a valid touch.
- 192 is INVALID.
- 254 is INVALID.
- 254.5 is INVALID.

A timestamp being inside the duration is only a boundary check. It is NOT
evidence that a touch occurred.

If the verified source contact time is beyond clip_duration_s, DELETE the touch.
Do not clamp, shift, wrap, reuse, or reinterpret the timestamp.

For example, if the actual video ends at 191 seconds and a candidate event is
at 254 seconds:

DO NOT convert 254 to 191.
DO NOT convert 254 to 153.
DO NOT keep the event.
DELETE THE TOUCH.

CONTACT-TIME RULE:

Use the actual moment of player-ball contact.

Do not use:

- action start time;
- action end time;
- player arrival time;
- ball departure time;
- follow-through time;
- next visible frame unless that frame is the actual contact frame;
- previous visible frame unless that frame is the actual contact frame;
- observation t0 or t1 unless that exact point is the verified contact.

NO TOUCH INTERPOLATION:

Never create touches between two verified touches.

Example:

Verified:
153
157

Do NOT add:
154
155
156

unless separate player-ball contacts are visibly confirmed at those times.

MULTIPLE TOUCHES:

A continuous carry can contain multiple touches. Do not represent a carry as
one touch merely because it is one observation.

Record only the individual contacts that are visibly verified.

A carry may therefore have:
- zero confirmed touches;
- one confirmed touch;
- multiple confirmed touches.

Do not invent additional touches to make a carry, pass, dribble, possession
sequence, or tally appear complete.

SAME-SECOND TOUCHES:

If two or more distinct verified contacts fall within the same integer second,
do not invent decimal timestamps to separate them.

The schema requires integer seconds.

Use the verified integer second for each confirmed contact, subject to the
application/database handling of repeated integer values.

ZONE RULE:

The touch zone must be the target player's actual zone at the verified contact
moment.

Do NOT copy the zone from:
- an earlier touch;
- a later touch;
- an observation that occurs at a different time;
- a carry;
- a shot;
- a presence span;
- a nearby frame;

unless the player's actual position at the verified contact moment is visibly
consistent with that zone.

If the player position at the contact moment cannot be reliably assigned to a
valid zone, do not invent a zone. Because "zone" is required by the schema,
do not create that touch record if its zone cannot be reliably established.

PRESENCE RULE:

The actual verified ball-contact moment must occur inside an "in" presence
span.

If the contact occurs during:
- out;
- off;
- uncertain identity;
- insufficient visibility;
- absent tracking where identity cannot be established;

the touch MUST NOT be recorded.

Do not move the touch to the nearest "in" second to make it fit.

NO GAP FILLING:

Do not fill missing touch intervals.

If verified touches occur at 153 and 157, the system must not assume there were
touches at 154, 155, or 156.

TOUCH COUNT INDEPENDENCE:

The number of touches is determined only by individually verified contacts.
Do not derive touch counts from:
- action_tallies;
- counts_only;
- observations count;
- number of passes;
- number of carries;
- number of possession sequences;
- report wording.

FINAL TOUCH VALIDATION — REQUIRED FOR EVERY TOUCH:

For each proposed touch, ask all of the following:

1. Is the target player clearly identified at the contact moment?
2. Is the ball sufficiently visible at the contact moment?
3. Is actual player-ball contact visibly confirmed?
4. Has the exact contact frame been identified?
5. Was the source time derived from that contact frame?
6. Is the source time within the actual video duration?
7. Does the resulting "t" satisfy 0 <= t <= clip_duration_s?
8. Is "t" a JSON integer with no decimal component?
9. Is the contact inside an "in" presence span?
10. Is the recorded zone the player's actual zone at that contact moment?
11. Is this a distinct visible contact rather than an inferred or duplicated one?
12. Was this touch established independently rather than created from another
    observation or action record?

If ANY answer is NO or uncertain:

DELETE THE TOUCH.

FAIL-CLOSED TOUCH POLICY:

It is better to output too few touches than to output one invented touch.

Never add a touch to make:
- observations complete;
- shots complete;
- passes complete;
- carries complete;
- dribbles complete;
- action tallies complete;
- counts_only complete;
- reports complete.

If no touch survives all checks, the correct output is:

"touches": []

Do not create a touch merely because a touch would be expected from football
logic.

FINAL QUESTION FOR EVERY TOUCH:

"Can I point to the actual video moment where the target player visibly
contacted the ball?"

If NO -> remove the touch.

If YES -> verify the exact contact frame, source time, integer timestamp,
presence state, and zone before keeping it.

6.11 REPORT-TO-EVIDENCE CONSISTENCY

This section is mandatory.

Before producing the final JSON, validate all of the following:

A. Timestamp validation

- Every timestamp in detailed_analysis must match an observation t0 or t0–t1,
  converted with the Section 3.3 formula.
- No unsupported timestamps are allowed.
- No report timestamp may be generated from memory.
- Do not cite a timestamp just because the player was visible at that time.
- No record may exceed clip_duration_s.

B. Action validation

- Every action described in the report must exist in observations.
- Every off-ball run claim must have an off_ball_run observation.
- Every positioning claim must have a positioning observation.
- Every pressing claim must have a press observation.
- Every recovery-run claim must have a recovery-run observation.
- Every tackle, interception, pass, dribble, carry, and shot claim must
  have its corresponding evidence.
- Do not describe an unrecorded action.

C. Strength validation

- Every strength must be supported by observations.
- Use "consistent" only when at least two independent records support the
  same behaviour.
- Do not infer first-touch quality without first-touch evidence.
- Do not infer passing quality without passing evidence.
- Do not infer defensive ability without defensive evidence.

D. Weakness validation

- Every weakness must be supported by an attempted but poorly executed
  action or a clearly poor decision.
- A not_observed category can never be a weakness.
- Lack of defensive observations must never become lack of defensive effort.
- Do not use "lack of pressing intensity" unless pressing was observed
  and its execution was demonstrably poor.
- Do not use "lack of physical development" or similar physical claims.

E. Shot validation

- Every shot must appear in observations.
- The shot outcome must agree in both places.
- The shot timestamp must be consistent.
- The corresponding touch must occur at the strike time.
- Do not give contradictory descriptions of the same shot.

F. Tally validation

- Reconcile attempted and successful counts with observed actions inside
  "in" spans. A tally may exceed the number of observation records for that
  action, but must never be lower than it.
- successful must never exceed attempted.
- Do not include an action tally with zero attempts.
- Do not count an action twice.
- No tally or count may include an action from outside an "in" span.

G. Presence validation

- Every observation, touch, shot, and zone must fall within an in span.
- No spans may overlap.
- No gaps are allowed between consecutive presence spans.
- The last span ends exactly at clip_duration_s.
- Short visibility flicker must be handled according to Section 6.2.
- If an observation or touch cannot be placed inside an in span, remove it, or
  correct the span when the footage clearly shows the player was in frame.
  Never leave a conflict between the presence spans and the evidence records.

H. Attribute validation

- Every Four Corner attribute must trace to evidence.
- No new action may be introduced during synthesis.
- Do not infer physical or psychological attributes.

I. Language and formatting validation

- No club or team names, countries, or shirt numbers.
- No invented player identity.
- No evaluative hype.
- No malformed headings.
- No merged headings and paragraphs.
- No contradictory English and Arabic evidence descriptions.
- No unsupported claims hidden inside long paragraphs.

If any validation check fails, correct the report or remove the unsupported
claim before returning the JSON.

  the tracking overlay is a cue. "Identification Limitations" must state the
  share of the clip when the player was not visible, computed from the
  presence spans (for example "not visible for about 61% of the clip").
  Do not quote span timestamps and do not guess causes such as replays,
  close-ups or camera angles.
- Use counts that match the records: one record is "one", never "runs",
  "options", "occasions" or "consistently". Do not describe movements such as
  "dropping", "between the lines" or "right half-space" unless a record's text
  and zone state them.
- Do not include unrecorded events.
- Do not repeat unsupported statements in different sections.
- Do not write generic scouting filler.

9. SUMMARY FORMATTING
==================================================

The summary must be self-contained and understandable without
detailed_analysis.

Use exactly this structure:

• Player:
  <player_description> (<confidence>)

• Profile:
  <2–3 sentences based only on observed evidence>

• Key Strengths:
  - <observed strength>
  - <observed strength>

• Areas for Improvement:
  - <observed weakness>

• Confidence:
  <what was observed confidently and any material evidence limitations>

• Rating:
  <score> / 10

• Recommendation:
  <short evidence-based recommendation>

Rules:

- The confidence in parentheses is the label (high / medium / low) for the
  identification confidence number, not the number.
- Use the supplied player_description verbatim, including any leading
  article such as "the" (write "the tracked player", never "tracked player"),
  with nothing appended
  (no country, number, or kit).
- Never invent a player name.
- Maximum 4 strengths.
- Maximum 4 improvement points.
- If no weakness was observed, write only:
  - None observed in this footage.
- Never turn a not_observed category into an improvement point.
- Do not claim consistent behaviour from one observation.
- Do not add a new timestamp or event.
- Do not add a new technical or tactical attribute.
- Do not include any H1 or H2 Markdown heading.
- Do not use paragraphs outside the specified blocks.
- Keep the two-space indent for the line under each bullet label, exactly as
  in the template, and write grammatical English.

==================================================
10. SCORE AND VERDICT
==================================================

score:

- Numeric float between 0.0 and 10.0.
- Use one decimal place.
- Must be consistent with the summary and detailed_analysis.
- Must be based on observed performance, not on the volume of actions alone.
- Do not penalise the player for categories that were not observed.
- Reduce confidence when the evidence is limited.
- If the evidence is insufficient for a reliable assessment, state that
  the rating is provisional in the summary.
- EVIDENCE CAP: if the clip has fewer than 15 observation records, or fewer
  than 3 of the 5 game phases are represented, the rating is provisional,
  score must not exceed 7.0, and verdict must be monitor.

verdict must be exactly one of:

sign
monitor
pass

SIGN:

Use only when multiple categories have sufficient positive evidence and
consistent patterns support a confident recruitment recommendation. Never
use sign when the evidence cap above applies.

PASS:

Use only when clear, repeated, well-supported negative evidence demonstrates
that the player does not fit the required standard.

Do not use pass because the footage lacks defensive actions or other evidence.

MONITOR:

Use when:

- Evidence is mixed.
- Evidence is limited.
- Important categories are not observed.
- Identification confidence is medium or low.
- The rating is provisional.
- There is insufficient evidence for a confident sign or pass decision.
- The content is not analyzable.

Consistency:

- sign should not be paired with a low score.
- pass should not be paired with a high score.
- monitor is the correct verdict for incomplete or inconclusive evidence.

The score, verdict, recommendation, strengths, weaknesses, and profile must
all tell the same evidence-supported story.
